# Extension of the Vietnamese reader-question evaluation

The earlier frozen 11-case cohort and its seven bridge questions are preserved. This extension samples published reader questions from press and citizen Q&A channels. We keep only a documented first-person or explicitly attributed travel decision, an independently published Vietnamese evidence passage, and a compatible decision scope. Generic company FAQs, generated prompts, individual account-support inquiries, and decisions without authoritative text are ineligible. Published attribution establishes provenance at the source level. Independent identity verification and fresh human ratings were outside this extension.

Some questions contain multiple decisions. The retained text is a contiguous source excerpt covering one decision. Preserve the original itinerary and conditions inside that span. Historical rules are used only for explicitly dated historical decisions where their scope can be corroborated. General policy questions without a dated event are replayed on the explicitly stated archived policy side. The archive is for measurement, not current travel advice.

The retrieval library combines the earlier 391 chunks with independent archived source windows. It does not include reader-question pages or the publisher's answer printed alongside the question. A source manifest records URLs and SHA-256 digests. Each new official text section is identified before scores. Select by BM25 unigram with project k1=1.5, b=0.75 and deterministic passage-ID tie-breaking. Freeze data and judgments before neural scoring. On confirmed misses, replace rank five with a previously verified sufficient passage. Unknown candidates are documented rather than silently counted as failures. Score the same candidates with pretrained BGE and three seeds of baseline M0 and augmentation M1 if available.

This purposive extension tests bounded decision evidence in a closed Vietnamese library. It yields case-level evidence and descriptive counts. It does not estimate the performance of users at large or the rate of hallucination in generated answers.
